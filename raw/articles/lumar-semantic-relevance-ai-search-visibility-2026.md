# Semantic Relevance Isn't One Metric — It Has Three Jobs

Source: https://www.lumar.io/blog/best-practice/semantic-relevance-ai-search-visibility/
Author: Lumar Editorial Team
Publish Date: 2026-08-21

## Reframing

Semantic relevance isn't one similarity score — it performs three
distinct evaluative jobs at different retrieval stages of AI search
systems. Reducing it to one metric obscures where content actually
succeeds or fails in the pipeline, leading to misguided optimization.

## The three jobs

1. **Page eligibility (candidate selection)** — "Is this page about the
   query?" Broad topical relevance is the priority here, not the
   strongest possible answer.
2. **Passage identification (chunk retrieval)** — "Which passage best
   answers this?" Precision increases: systems demand specific
   entities, units, and clear phrasing at this stage.
3. **Citation suitability** — content must be understandable
   independently, without depending on surrounding context or
   undefined references, to be safely quoted/cited.

## Key claims

- Relevance operates in stages; each stage needs a different
  evaluation approach and thus a different optimization response.
- Some highly relevant pages still fail to contribute to AI responses
  because they don't succeed across all three stages — passing one
  doesn't guarantee passing the others.
- No quantified data/statistics in this piece — conceptual analysis
  based on observed AI retrieval patterns and IR theory, not an
  empirical study.

## Recommended tactics

1. Establish topical clarity — structure pages so the primary subject
   is immediately identifiable (page-eligibility stage).
2. Keep sections narrowly focused so individual passages match
   specific queries closely (chunk-retrieval stage).
3. Prioritize passage independence — chunks must communicate complete
   information without requiring additional page context (citation
   stage).
4. Treat these as three separate optimization targets, not one.
