# How to identify content gaps for AI visibility

Source: https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/ai-content-gaps
Author: Cassie Wilson Clark (HubSpot)
Updated: 2026-10-23
Ingested: 2026-10-25
Note: saved from a WebFetch summary (paraphrase, not verbatim).

## Core idea
An AI visibility content gap = a relevant question (or group) where a brand is absent from AI answers, lacks supporting citations, or has no content that adequately answers it. Differs from SEO gaps: a page can rank in Google yet be missing from AI answers.

## Three gap types
- Coverage gap: no page adequately answers the question.
- Extractability gap: page exists but useful info is hard to find/interpret/reuse.
- Citation-support gap: brand mentioned or has content, but another source supplies the evidence the AI uses.

## Four-step process
1. Collect prompt data, grouped by topic and intent, across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, etc.
2. Classify mentions and citations separately (prompt, platform, date, brands, sources).
3. Map prompts to existing content: coverage, clarity of answer, evidence, audience/decision-criteria match.
4. Prioritize: business impact vs. effort; high-impact/low-effort first; defer low-impact/high-effort.

## Analyzing / improving
Review AI answers and cited sources (without copying competitors) for needed information, evidence, framing. Pages: answer directly and early; descriptive headings; related claims near evidence; define terms, consistent terminology; key info in text not only images; add original signals (firsthand experience, documented examples, research with methodology).

## Choosing an action
Refresh outdated pages; improve extractability; strengthen evidence; create new content only if no suitable page; defer peripheral/out-of-capability/weakly evidenced gaps and record why.

## Measurement
Brand mention presence (answers mentioning brand / tracked answers); citation presence (answers citing >=1 owned URL / tracked answers); AI share of voice (entity- or citation-based, pick one); AI-referred traffic and outcomes (not proof of causation). Baseline first, same prompts/platforms, expect run-to-run variation.

## Pitfalls
Chasing every missing mention; broad topics vs high-impact ones; over-optimizing structure at expense of readability; treating AI drafts as finished without fact-checking.
