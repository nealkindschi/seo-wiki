# Does topical focus make your brand more visible?

**Publication Date:** August 3, 2026
**Author:** Kevin Indig (Growth Memo)

## Executive Summary

Examines whether topical authority — concentrating content strategy
around core expertise areas — improves brand visibility in AI search.
Using ChatGPT data across ~1,000 categories, finds that topical
authority works differently for **citations** vs. **named mentions**,
with significant industry-specific variation.

## Methodology

- **Data source**: Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit (US ChatGPT, Jan-June
  2026).
- **Sample**: 1,094 categories, 5 prompt variants per category, 283,215
  citation observations, 76,493 named-mention observations, 45,578
  expansion appearances by 1,458 brand entities (Jan-May 2026).
- **Measures**: citation share (domain's proportion of source citations
  in category answers), brand-mention share (proportion of named brand
  mentions), depth (number of prompt variants, 1-5, where a brand
  appears), breadth (number of categories where a brand appears).
- **Controls**: current share, current depth, Authority Score, organic
  traffic, branded search demand, category-month differences, industry
  classification.
- **Stated limitation**: measures associations only; cannot prove
  publishing/strategy changes caused the outcomes.

## Key findings

### 1. Citations vs. mentions behave differently by category relevance

- **In distant categories** (outside core expertise): 50% of
  appearances are citations, only 25% are named mentions, 9% get both.
- **In closely related categories**: 74% are cited, 44% are named
  mentions, 34% get both.
- **Citation-only presence is stable regardless of relevance** (41%
  distant vs. 40% close).
- "You can be a source on pretty much any topic if it matches regular
  criteria for source citations. But the AI recommends you in core
  topics."

### 2. Depth matters more than breadth

- Appearing across many categories without depth shows a negative
  association with brand mentions (-0.051 at 1-of-5 prompt variants),
  reversing to slightly positive at full depth (5-of-5 variants).
- Citation share shows minimal spread-thin penalty across breadth
  levels (+0.012 to +0.062).
- "The spread-thin penalty is really a penalty for shallow presence,
  not for breadth itself."

### 3. Industry-specific authority effects

- **Finance & Real Estate** (strong breadth effect): Finance's
  citation-breadth association rises from +0.054 to +0.139; Real
  estate from +0.021 to +0.135. Broader coverage strengthens source
  visibility and supports expansion strategies in these sectors.
- **Legal & Healthcare** (tight constraints): Legal's mention-breadth
  association stays negative (-0.058) even at full coverage; Healthcare
  -0.037. "Being a credible source is not enough to make a brand the
  recommended answer" in high-stakes fields.

### 4. Category ownership requires repeat mentions, not just citations

"A category is not won until the brand earns repeat mentions, not just
citations."

### Relatedness test

Brands demonstrating expertise (3+ of 5 prompt variants) in an
established category show a measurable but light advantage when
expanding into related categories.

## Tactical recommendations

1. **Finance/Real Estate teams**: use citations as an early success
   metric for expansion into related categories.
2. **Legal/Healthcare teams**: require demonstrated repeat mentions
   (not just citations) before declaring category ownership.
3. **Build category maps**: identify authority anchors, score category
   closeness, and separate source opportunities (citations) from
   recommendation opportunities (mentions).
4. **Prioritize depth over breadth**: become truly established
   (appearing across multiple prompt variants) in core categories
   before expanding broadly.

## Conclusion

Topical focus produces conditional visibility benefits: brands with
demonstrated authority in core topics expand more successfully into
adjacent areas for citations, but named-mention benefits require both
topical relevance *and* sufficient category depth. The advantage is
strongest in finance/real estate and weakest in legal/healthcare.
