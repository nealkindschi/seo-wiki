## Thesis
Niche selection is a commercial bet on a specific, painful problem for a specific buyer group, not a passion project. "Fitness" / "Pet care" are containers, not niches. A good niche must be discoverable in both classic search and AI-driven discovery.

## 1. Beyond "follow your passion"
- Passion sustains work, doesn't validate demand. Strong niches = specific pain in large evergreen markets.
- Cites a community discussion suggesting Google paid ads signal commercial viability.
- Weak -> strong: "Small businesses" -> "Multi-location dental practices"; "Get more traffic" -> "Recover lost local visibility after listing inconsistency".
- Four filters: painful enough to trigger action? buyer describable in one sentence? commercial signals visible? can the topic earn trust in AI-mediated discovery?

## 2. Uncovering and vetting ideas
- "Market forensics" starting from buyer friction. Sources: review sites (G2, Capterra), sales calls, support tickets, autocomplete / People Also Ask, active communities.
- Quick screen: paid ads, specific pain language, visible content gaps, audience concentration, AI search visibility (AI Overviews, citations, vendor mentions).
- Shortlist: one sentence each for buyer, painful trigger, offer, proof channel; kill ideas where a line stays vague.

## 3. Quantifying opportunity
- Four-week research phase (opportunity, market sizing, TAM). Weeks 1-2: keywords from Semrush/Ahrefs/GSC/autocomplete grouped by intent (problem-aware, solution-aware, transaction-adjacent, authority); review SERPs (who ranks, formats, forums, AIO patterns). Week 3: TAM from narrow audience, then realistic share given competition, channel access, sales model.
- Approval doc: demand breadth, SERP quality, audience reachability, credible revenue path. Weak analysis: "lots of searches", "industry is growing".

## 4. Validating demand before building
- Real validation = action (email signups, add-to-cart, DMs), not page views. Smoke test: one hypothesis, one-page offer, small paid campaign (e.g. Meta), track intent signals.
- Landing page: specific headline, pain-focused copy, offer framing, single CTA, fast.
- Clear intent -> proceed; interest w/o action -> refine positioning; no response -> reconsider. Testing privately protects crowded niches from early exposure.

## 5. Content and monetization alignment
- Cites research that specialized segments yield higher margins; UK higher-ed niche sales +95% 2010-2015 (£166.2M -> £324.2M, Taylor & Francis).
- Niche types: informational service (consulting/retainers/courses/affiliates), product (direct sales/bundles/subscriptions), community (sponsorships/memberships/premium).
- Alignment check: niche type, first conversion, supporting content asset, content to avoid. Avoid top-funnel articles with no commercial bridge; don't mix monetization models before one proves out.

## 6. AI search future-proofing
- Cites 68% of consumers using AI assistants for initial product research (secondary source).
- Checks: AIO representation, entity clarity, citation gaps (weak/outdated sources a specialist could replace), assistant discoverability (broad brands vs focused experts).
- Some niches look crowded in classic SERPs but have thin AI-cited sources (opening); others compress into a few dominant AI-cited brands (less attractive). Validate demand and discoverability together.

## Practical tactics
Replace category labels with one-sentence buyer+problem; mine reviews/support/communities; paid ads + SERP review as commercial screens; 4-week sprint with TAM; smoke test; match content to niche type; check AIO/assistant visibility before committing.
