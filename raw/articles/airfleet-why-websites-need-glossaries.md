# Why Every Website Needs a Glossary (paraphrase)

## Benefits claimed
- Long-tail search traffic: each entry can target specific, less
  competitive questions.
- Internal linking to blog posts, guides, product pages; helps search
  engines map the site and distributes authority.
- Featured snippets: concise definitions suit the snippet format —
  caveat noted by the author: assumes the page already ranks on page one.
- Topical authority from thorough subject coverage.
- UX: easy navigation, direct answers, lower bounce rate.
- Voice search: Q&A formatting fits voice queries.
- Long-term value: a maintained glossary grows with the business.

## Building the glossary
Answer real user questions; use customer language from sales calls,
support chats, and transcripts; pair terms with search queries; link to
deeper content; split large glossaries by topic.

## Research tools
Semrush, Ahrefs; AnswerThePublic, AlsoAsked; Google Search Console;
customer conversation logs. Target terms with reasonable volume and
manageable competition.

## Maintenance
Review definitions quarterly; add new terms as the industry changes;
fix broken internal links; track performance in Search Console.

## UX design
A–Z navigation, accordion-style definitions, search function, a
consistent entry format (term, plain-English explanation, optional CTA
or link), full mobile support.

## Case studies (unnamed clients, no figures)
1. Added FAQ schema markup; traffic gains within the month.
2. A 90+ page glossary grew steadily after similar optimization.
3. A glossary launched in April showed continued organic growth four
   months later.

## Conclusion
Treat the glossary as a long-term content hub answering real questions,
not an internal dictionary.
