# How to Grow Your AI Visibility with Community Signals

Source: https://www.growth-memo.com/p/community-signals-are-ais-largest
Authors: Kevin Indig and Amanda Johnson
Publish Date: 2026-08-10

## Key claims

1. **UGC dominance in AI citations**: UGC platforms surpass review
   sites and publishers combined across all buyer-journey stages for
   SaaS-related prompts.
2. **Flat trajectory**: unlike review platforms (which spike during
   evaluation stages), UGC citation presence stays flat (17-18%)
   regardless of purchase-funnel position.
3. **Platform concentration**: Wikipedia, Reddit, and LinkedIn = 99% of
   UGC citations, yet individual platforms are highly volatile even
   while the aggregate stays stable.
4. **Freshness correlation**: pages refreshed within 6 months dominate
   high-intent citations.

## Data & statistics

- UGC citation share: 17.1% of cited domains (vendor domains excluded).
- Publisher share: 4.0% — UGC is ~4x larger.
- Wikipedia alone: 10.1-14.0 percentage points of the 17-point floor.
- 83% of commercial-query citations come from pages updated in the
  past 12 months.
- Stale pages are 3x more likely to lose AI citations.
- Sequential heading structure (H2>H3>H4) → 2.8x citation increase.
- Only 30% of brand visibility persists between separate AI responses
  (high volatility run-to-run).

## Methodology

~35,000 citation URLs from Profound. US-only, ChatGPT only, December
2025. Prompts classified: 376 discovery, 1,179 exploration, 1,367
evaluation, 255 focused evaluation. Deduplicated one per run/intent/
domain.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors**: single-engine (ChatGPT
only) analysis; the broader "consensus gap" (91% of citations appear in
only one engine) means this doesn't generalize automatically to other
engines; prompts were vendor-seeking by design (vendor domains hold
66.7-71.8% of citations, UGC/publisher shares are of the remainder);
aggregate UGC measurement masks real platform-level volatility.

## Platform-specific findings

- **Wikipedia**: largest third-party source but least actionable —
  requires accurate upstream sourcing for editors to cite it correctly.
- **Reddit**: most volatile, but worth prioritizing — declined 11.7%
  visibility / 10.9% mentions in one 28-day window, then recovered
  18.1% three weeks later.
- **LinkedIn**: named individuals outperform brand accounts; showed
  43.3% SEO-visibility gain in one 28-day period.
- **YouTube**: durable (Google-owned, more controllable); showed 11%
  growth relative to other platforms.

## Eight actionable recommendations

1. Identify which UGC platforms feed your category via high-intent
   prompt testing.
2. Highlight named people over brand accounts in community spaces.
3. Answer recurring support-ticket questions directly on community
   platforms.
4. Embed third-party review quotes (with source links) in the top 30%
   of your most-cited pages.
5. Track the aggregate UGC citation floor and rebalance platform
   composition quarterly.
6. Mine communities for audience terminology and questions.
7. Correct outdated thread information with dated updates and links.
8. Provide customer-worthy assets (early data access, free tools) for
   organic sharing.

## Critical distinction

Separate the **aggregate UGC floor** (~17%, the number to defend) from
**platform composition** (the Wikipedia/Reddit/LinkedIn mix, which
should be actively rebalanced quarterly). Category visibility requires
portfolio thinking across platforms, not a single-platform bet.

## Measurement gap

Google Search Console's July 2026 "platform properties" feature covers
Instagram, TikTok, X, and YouTube — but excludes Reddit and LinkedIn,
the two highest-performing UGC platforms in this analysis.
