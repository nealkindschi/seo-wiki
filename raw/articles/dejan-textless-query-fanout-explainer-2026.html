Textless Query Fan-Out

Google Research paper · arXiv 2603.06397 · March 2026

Query fan-out without text

How query fan-out would change if Google adopted the paper's R4T-Diffusion model. Before: an LLM writes sub-queries as text, and the text is searched. After: a small diffusion model generates vectors, and search runs on those vectors directly.

Setup as reported in the paper. Every fan-out method there produces 10 sub-queries or vectors per query. Timings are wall-clock fan-out time per batch (Figure 5).

The LLM moves to training time

  1. Train a fan-out LLM with reinforcement learning. Rewards score whole sets of sub-queries, for example on diversity, relevance to the query and closeness to real items.
  2. Sample it offline. 128 fan-outs per training query, at temperature 0.9. The vectors behind those fan-outs become training targets.
  3. Train the diffusion model to produce those target vectors from the query vector. At search time only this 53.9M-parameter model runs.

The distilled model keeps most of the LLM's diversity: 74.3 against 76.8 (Polyvore, LLM-judged diversity, Gemma3-4B teacher).

What the paper tested

Polyvore fashion outfits and a proprietary set of expert music playlists. It did not test web search, and it does not say Google uses R4T in Search, AI Overviews or AI Mode. The After pipeline on this page shows what Google's fan-out would look like with the paper's method.

Source: Jiang et al., Efficient, Property-Aligned Fan-Out Retrieval via RL-Compiled Diffusion, Google Research and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, arXiv 2603.06397, March 2026.
