# Why AI Gets Your Brand Details Wrong—and How to Fix It

**Publication Date:** June 22, 2026
**Author:** Rebecca Brosnan (seoClarity)

## Executive Summary

AI systems frequently misrepresent brand information. Unlike
traditional search optimization focused on owned properties, AI search
requires managing information across multiple sources — when AI
encounters incomplete, inconsistent, or conflicting brand data, it
fills gaps with inaccurate information, potentially damaging customer
trust and business performance.

## Four core problems causing AI inaccuracy

1. **Missing information** — incomplete website content forces AI to
   supplement from other sources or make assumptions.
2. **Positioning misalignment** — AI correctly identifies what a
   company does but misunderstands target audience or market
   positioning.
3. **Unstructured data** — critical facts buried in unformatted content
   rather than machine-readable markup.
4. **Conflicting external sources** — outdated directories, forums, and
   competitor content create a contradictory information ecosystem.

## Illustrative examples

- Phone-plan pricing conflicts between an AI Overview response and a
  ChatGPT response, indicating unclear company documentation.
- A Home Depot location showing 6am-10pm hours on its own website, but
  AI displaying a 9pm closing time.
- A luxury brand (Balsam Hill) described by AI as providing less
  "value" than competitors.

## Recommendations

**Missing information**: publish comprehensive, factual content
answering common customer questions; develop detailed product
information, service descriptions, and FAQs; reduce ambiguity through
authoritative source documentation.

**Positioning clarity**: audit brand descriptions across owned sites,
press releases, analyst reports, and third-party mentions; maintain
consistent language regarding audience, market position, expertise, and
differentiators; use sentiment-analysis tooling to track brand
perception across AI platforms.

**Data structure**: implement schema markup (product schema,
organization schema) for machine-readable context; structure pricing,
specifications, dates, and service offerings explicitly. "Structured
data [is] a way of explicitly telling machines which information
represents the official version of the truth."

**External narrative management**: conduct regular audits of
third-party mentions; update outdated business listings; monitor the
broader information ecosystem surrounding brand presence; implement
ongoing monitoring systems.

## Broader framing

AI accuracy requires cross-functional collaboration across SEO,
content, PR, and data teams — "closer to an information governance
challenge" than a traditional marketing problem. Core argument: "In the
era of AI search, mentions get you seen. Accuracy earns trust."
