# Why Ranking on Page One Isn't Enough

**Publication Date:** July 18, 2025
**Author:** Oshen Davidson (AirOps)

## Executive Summary

While traditional SEO rankings remain a baseline requirement, AI-powered
answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google SGE) demand higher
structural standards. "Pages with rich schema are 13% more likely to
earn AI citations. Sequential heading structures boost citation odds
by 2.8x. Content that contains clear, easy-to-read sections drive
nearly half of AI citations."

## Methodology

- 900 high-intent queries across 15 industries, split evenly between
  informational and commercial intent.
- 12,000+ unique URLs evaluated.
- Comparison groups: ChatGPT-cited URLs vs. Google page-one results for
  the *identical* queries.
- Deliberately excluded off-page factors (backlinks, authority) to
  isolate controllable on-page structural elements.
- Focus question: why do some pages get cited in ChatGPT while
  high-ranking Google articles remain invisible?

## Structural attributes analyzed

1. **Heading structure** — single-H1 presence; proper sequential
   heading alignment (H1→H2→H3, no skipped levels).
2. **Schema implementation** — overall schema presence; FAQ/QAPage
   schema specifically; rich schema (3+ distinct types).
3. **List and bullet organization** — HTML ordered-list presence;
   unordered bullet-point sections; average count of each per page.

## Key findings

### Schema
- 61% of ChatGPT-cited pages contained rich schema (3+ types) vs. 25%
  of Google SERP URLs.
- FAQ/QAPage schema appeared in 10.5% of cited content vs. 5.4% of top
  Google results.

### Heading structure
- 68.7% of ChatGPT-cited URLs followed sequential heading structure vs.
  23.9% of Google top results — nearly 3x as likely in cited content.
- 87% of cited pages had a single H1 vs. 64% of Google SERP leaders.

### Lists
- 79% of ChatGPT-cited URLs included at least one list section vs.
  28.6% of Google top results.
- ChatGPT-cited pages averaged 13.75 distinct list sections — over 17x
  higher than the Google SERP average.

### Bullet points
- 72.9% of ChatGPT-cited pages contained bullet-point sections vs.
  74.5% of Google results — essentially equal, not a differentiator.
- Average sentence length nearly identical: 17.32 characters
  (ChatGPT-cited) vs. 16.54 (Google) — structure differentiates more
  than sentence-level simplicity.

### Forum/UGC discrepancy
- Discussion forums (Reddit, Quora) comprised 11.1% of Google page-one
  results but only 0.05% of ChatGPT citations for the same queries.

## Recommendations

1. Prioritize multi-type schema (3+ types: Article, FAQ, QAPage,
   BreadcrumbList, Person) over single-type implementation.
2. Enforce sequential heading hierarchy — exactly one H1, properly
   nested H2s/H3s, no skipped levels.
3. Increase list density — replace narrative text with numbered/
   ordered lists for step sequences, rankings, or hierarchies.
4. Use extractable formatting — scannable bullets/lists answer engines
   can easily lift.
5. "SEO best practices remain essential — but they're now just the
   starting point; answer engine visibility requires a higher standard
   of structure, clarity, and extractability."

## Case study: RentSpree

"The best online rent payment systems" article, cited for the query
"What platforms automate rent payment processing?" Structural strengths
identified: rich schema, single H1, proper heading alignment, editorial
bullet points throughout key sections.

## Key insight

Ranking on page one is "table stakes" but insufficient for AI
visibility — structurally weak pages regularly fail AI citation despite
strong Google rankings. "The same on-page attributes that enhance user
experience are strongly associated with increased citation in LLM and
answer engine results." Discussion platforms lack direct citation but
retain value for brand mentions and organic discoverability.
