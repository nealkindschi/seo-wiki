## Data and methodology

- 13-question survey run by Wynter (data shared by Peep Laja's team). Respondents shared their first AI prompt and follow-up sequence for a real software purchase.
- n = 200. All reported industry profiles included SaaS/software.
- Unweighted percentages; denominators shown per chart. No fieldwork dates given.
- Questions: what they were buying; buying stage at chat start; AI tool(s) used; how much the AI knew about their situation; vendors in mind beforehand; whether AI introduced a new shortlisted vendor; first message text; what the first message included; number of follow-ups; follow-up text; follow-up topics; whether the chosen vendor was in the AI's first answer; what they did after the chat.
- Demo/signup rate by tool: Claude 35.2% (50/142), ChatGPT 31.2% (39/125), Gemini 33.8% (26/77). All three had a median of 3–5 follow-ups. Groups overlap, so outcomes can't be attributed to one tool.
- Context: 137/200 (68.5%) selected "Fresh chat, no extra context"; 105 (52.5%) selected only that. Projects/saved instructions 26%, uploaded files 21%, continued chats 16.5%, not sure 1.5%. Account memory not measured.

## AI introduces new options

- Before the chat, 158/200 (79%) had ≥1 vendor in mind; 40 (20%) had none; 2 (1%) conflicting.
- Of the 158 with candidates, 99 (62.7%) added another vendor via AI. 71 of those 99 (72%) reported choosing a vendor; 28 undecided. 37 reported demo/signup (26 choosers, 11 undecided).
- Among all 158 with candidates, 69.0% reported a choice.
- Of the 40 with no candidates, AI added a shortlisted vendor for 33 (82.5%). Only 40% of that group reported choosing.
- Demo/signup: 25.0% (no-candidate group) vs 36.1% (with-candidates group).
- ~Two-thirds of the no-candidate group were exploring the category; ~half of the with-candidates group already had a shortlist.
- Cites McKinsey: most buyers reach a decision with ~2 options in mind.
- Examples: an engineer evaluating AI visibility tools restated a requirement ("product level insights, not just brand level"); an LMS-researching PM asked AI for non-vendor-posted reviews (e.g. G2).
- Author takeaway: build awareness before buyers are in-market (ads, creators, social, email), then earn recommendation in AI answers once research starts. Counting mentions is only part of the picture.

## Buyers use AI to evaluate software products

- 41.5% sent 3–5 follow-ups after the first prompt; one-third sent 6+.
- Of 126 who chose a vendor: 78 (62%) said it appeared in the first AI answer; 43 (34%) said it appeared after follow-ups. 75 of the 78 still sent follow-ups.
- Demo/signup: 41.0% (32/78) first-answer choosers vs 34.9% (15/43) later-appearing — not isolated as a causal effect.
- Example: a buyer asked AI to eliminate vendors lacking a Marketo API integration.
- Follow-up topics: pricing 57.5%, vendor comparisons 54.5%, integrations 46%, setup 40%.
- Topic breadth: 1–2 follow-ups → avg 2.4 topics; 10+ → avg 5.0.
- 10+ message chats asked about pricing, comparisons, implementation, and for a single recommendation.
- Demo/signup: 41.8% for 6+ follow-ups vs 32.3% for 1–5; peaks at 6–10, falls at 10+.
- Author takeaway: buyers use AI to eliminate products, assess trade-offs, and prepare internal decisions; buyers who state integrations, team size and use case get sharper recommendations.

## AI research complements Google

- After the chat: 145/200 (72.5%) visited vendor websites; 96 (48.0%) Googled; asked peers 31.0%; searched Reddit 29.0%; checked G2/review sites 23.0%.
- Not recorded: what was Googled, how vendor sites were reached, order of activities (a site visit may have been the signup itself).
- 67.5% used more than one AI platform. Claude 71%, ChatGPT 62.5%, Gemini 38.5%; 15% selected Google AI Overviews or AI Mode.

## Win the buyer before the chat starts

- Recap: 63% of those with vendors added another via AI; 62% of choosers picked a first-answer vendor and 75/78 still followed up; post-chat 73% visited vendor sites, 48% Googled.
- Ties to the author's "retrieved → cited → trusted" pipeline: retrieval sets the candidate set, the model selects citations, users decide which to trust and act on.
- Author takeaway: build familiarity before the chat, earn consideration in AI answers, make pricing, integrations and product fit easy to evaluate.

## Caveats (stated in source)

Self-reported, unweighted, not representative of all B2B buyers; some personal/non-purchasing accounts included; prompts recalled; demo/signup ≠ purchase; can't show whether an AI-introduced vendor was the one chosen or establish causality; overlapping tool groups; account memory unmeasured; chat length and first-answer inclusion are correlations; author invested in Wynter.
