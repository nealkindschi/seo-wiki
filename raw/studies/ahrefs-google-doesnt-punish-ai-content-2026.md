# Google Doesn't Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (331k Pages Studied)

**Publication Date:** July 27, 2026
**Author:** Ryan Law, Director of Content Marketing at Ahrefs
(contributor: Xibeijia Guan)

## Executive Summary

Challenges the notion that Google systematically penalizes AI-generated
content. Fully AI-written pages do rank highly; overall performance
correlates with content quality rather than AI detection alone.

## Methodology

- 1,000,000 pages from top-10 SERP positions across 100,000 searches
  (June 2026).
- 300,000 pages available in Ahrefs' crawler database; 150,000 pages
  had sufficient content (350+ words) for AI detection.
- AI detection via Ahrefs' AI content detector (Site Audit/Site
  Explorer).
- Classification: Low (<20% AI), Moderate (20-50%), High (50-80%), Very
  High (≥80%).
- Indexation analysis: 100,000 sampled pages; indexed status confirmed
  via organic keyword rankings, GSC impressions (since Jan 2026), or
  Google `site:` search.
- Performance tracking: GSC data from 80,861 live URLs across two
  windows (June 2025-Jan 2026 and Jan-June 2026).
- Stated limitation: "AI content detection is not perfect, and the way
  we detect AI content will be different from how Google does."

## Key findings

### AI content in top rankings
- 5.3% of top-3 pages are entirely AI-generated.
- 9% of top-3 positions contain ≥80% AI content.
- 82.2% of top-3 rankings have under 50% AI content.
- 54.7% of top-ranking pages have less than 20% AI content.

### AI distribution across SERP positions
- Between 8.4% (position 1) and 11.7% (position 10) of pages contain
  ≥80% AI content across all ranking positions.
- Average AI content: 27.1% (position 1) → 30.9% (position 10).
- Median AI content: 17.1% (position 1) → 19.5% (position 10).

### Indexation rates by AI content level
- Low: 49.28%. Moderate: 43.38%. High: 40.72%. Very High: 40.35%.
- Despite the lower rate, 40% of very-high-AI-content pages remain
  indexed — no hard cutoff.

### Organic performance
- Low/moderate AI-content pages received 2-3x more impressions than
  high/very-high AI-content pages.
- High/very-high AI-content pages showed stable impressions over time —
  no precipitous drop-off pattern for highly AI-generated content as a
  class.

## Claims about Google's treatment of AI content

Google's official position: "Automation has long been used in
publishing to create useful content. AI can assist with and generate
useful content in exciting new ways."

**Primary conclusion**: Google doesn't specifically target AI-generated
content for demotion; quality standards apply universally. Evidence:
fully AI-generated content exists at all ranking positions; no hard
cutoffs suggesting binary AI classification blocks indexation;
performance decline correlates with quality, not AI use alone.

**Secondary insight**: AI-generated content typically exhibits quality
issues that independently harm ranking — repetition of common knowledge
without new value, missing internal/external links/images/visuals,
academic-style writing without personality, factual errors.

**The "Mount AI" phenomenon**: traffic spikes followed by dramatic
drops are attributed to Google's crawl economics, not an AI penalty:
"Google might initially burst-crawl the new setup out of curiosity. But
if the domain lacks baseline authority to sustain scale, Google
throttles resource allocation." Low-quality scaled content gets
naturally deprioritized without Google specifically targeting "AI."

## Author's position

"I don't think Google is trying to punish AI-generated content; I think
it is relying on the same old hallmarks of content quality that it
always has." Quality problems "commonly co-occur with AI use, but they
are not guaranteed by AI use" — advocates against fear of AI while
warning against spam/scaled content specifically.
