# Link Building Outreach Tactics

Why/when to use this: apply when running an active link-acquisition
campaign — prospecting targets, doing guest posting or resource-page
outreach, or diagnosing why an outreach process isn't converting. This is
the tactical layer under [[link-building]]'s conceptual framing (the four
ways links get built, quality metrics). Based on
[[pitchbox-link-prospecting-hacks]], [[ahrefs-link-building]], and
[[crawling-mondays-link-building-outreach-2020]].

## Prospect qualification: five questions

Per [[pitchbox-link-prospecting-hacks]], before pitching a site, check:

1. **Metrics standards** — domain authority, organic traffic, site health.
2. **Relevance** — topical alignment with your audience.
3. **Content uniqueness** — does your pitch offer genuinely new,
   complementary information (not a rehash of what's already there)?
4. **Linking likelihood** — target blog posts and resource pages;
   deprioritize forums and sales pages, which rarely link out.
5. **Freshness** — prioritize recently published, actively maintained
   content over stale pages.

Underlying principle: "links are a two-way street" — a good prospect gets
something of value from linking to you, not just a favor granted.

## Prospecting strategies

### Skyscraper 2.0
An extension of the classic Skyscraper technique (find what ranks, make
something better, pitch it to everyone linking to the original):

1. Mine backlinks from articles that directly compete with your target
   content.
2. Expand to **slightly adjacent topics** with related keywords — don't
   stop at exact-match competitors.

Example: a WooCommerce campaign for "How to create an online store" also
targeted sites linking to dropshipping guides, side-hustle articles, and
Etsy tutorials — the adjacent-topic expansion alone surfaced 213 additional
prospects beyond the exact-match set.

### Guest posting
- Don't rely solely on search-operator prospecting (e.g. `"write for us"
  keyword`) — it surfaces the same well-trodden sites everyone else finds,
  many of which are fee-charging gatekeepers, and misses untapped sites.
- Use long-tail keyword variations (3+ words) to surface less-obvious
  targets.
- "Piggy-back" competitors' backlink profiles: if a competitor got a guest
  post placement somewhere, that site is a proven guest-posting prospect
  for you too.
- Bridge to adjacent industries deliberately (e.g. a credit card company
  pitching education blogs, not just finance blogs).

### Resource page link building
- Use search operators to locate resource/links pages in your niche.
- Filter for quality (minimum DR, organic traffic) before pitching.
- Mine authority sites' own backlink profiles via a backlink tool's
  "links"/"resource" URL filters to find resource pages that already link
  to comparable content.

## Outreach process: keys to success

Per [[crawling-mondays-link-building-outreach-2020]] (Carrie Rose, Rise at
Seven; Gisele Navarro, NeoMam Studios):

- **Relationships beat cold volume.** Prior relationships with journalists,
  bloggers, and site owners convert far better than one-off cold pitches —
  invest in the relationship before you need something from it.
- **Have something worth linking to first.** Outreach amplifies a genuinely
  link-worthy asset; it can't manufacture demand for a mediocre one. This
  matches [[moz-beginners-guide-link-building]]'s framing that all link
  campaigns need "something of value to build links to."
- **Authority and referral traffic are different currencies.** Don't judge
  a placement purely by click-through volume — a high-authority placement
  can be worth pursuing even with modest direct traffic. Don't judge it
  purely by authority either if referral traffic and audience fit matter
  for the specific campaign goal.

## Common mistakes that break link building processes

Per [[crawling-mondays-link-building-outreach-2020]]:

- Treating outreach as a numbers game (mass, unpersonalized emails)
  instead of a relationship-and-relevance game — this is the "ends
  justify the means" failure mode that damages sender reputation and
  response rates for everyone in the space over time.
- Skipping prospect qualification (see the five questions above) and
  pitching sites that were never going to link regardless of pitch
  quality.

## Realistic benchmarks

- Cold outreach: expect roughly a 5% success rate from 100 emails sent
  ([[ahrefs-link-building]]) — budget volume and qualification accordingly
  rather than treating a low raw response rate as a sign the campaign is
  broken.
- Automation + quality filtering (e.g. deduplication across prospecting
  tools) is what makes prospecting at scale tractable — per
  [[pitchbox-link-prospecting-hacks]], the goal is to "save literally
  hundreds of hours per month" of manual list-building, not to skip
  qualification.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every prospect passes the five qualification questions before
      outreach.
- [ ] Prospecting covers both exact-match competitors and adjacent-topic
      sites (Skyscraper 2.0), not just direct competitors.
- [ ] Guest-post targets are sourced from competitor backlink profiles and
      long-tail queries, not just generic search operators.
- [ ] The pitch leads with a genuinely link-worthy asset, not a generic
      "please link to us" ask.
- [ ] Outreach is personalized and prioritizes existing/prior
      relationships over cold-list volume.
- [ ] Placements are evaluated on both authority and (where relevant)
      referral-traffic fit — not authority alone.
- [ ] Backlink anchor text is not being engineered/manipulated — see
      [[link-and-anchor-text-best-practices]].
