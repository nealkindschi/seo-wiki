Why / when to use this: a link-acquisition tactic to run alongside
content-driven link earning — use it when you need targeted, high-
acceptance-rate backlink opportunities rather than broad outreach.
Ranks as the 5th most popular link-building tactic among professionals
per [[ahrefs-broken-link-building]]. Complements
[[link-and-anchor-text-best-practices]] (which covers the on-site side
of link equity) and [[digital-pr-strategy]] (a different link-earning
mechanism — media coverage rather than dead-link replacement).

## What it is

Find a page that used to exist (now 404/5xx) but still has external
backlinks pointing to it, then persuade the linking site's owner to
redirect that link to your relevant content instead. Mutually
beneficial: the site owner fixes a broken link on their own page, you
gain a backlink.

## 4-Step process (tool-agnostic, per [[ahrefs-broken-link-building]])

1. **Find broken pages with backlinks.** Four discovery tactics, use
   more than one:
   - **Competitor analysis** — pull a competitor's "best by links"
     report, filter to 404s, sort by referring-domain count.
   - **Topic-wide search** — search a link-analysis tool's content
     index for broken pages about your subject with 20+ referring
     domains, regardless of who originally owned the page.
   - **Outbound-link analysis** — check competitors' own broken
     *outgoing* links; they've effectively pre-vetted this prospect
     list already by having linked to it themselves.
   - **Resource-page mining** — Google search for resource/links pages
     (`"useful links" + topic`, `intitle:"useful resources"`), then
     scan for dead links with a browser extension.
2. **Vet the prospects.** Filter for dofollow links, exclude
   subdomains, apply minimum domain-rating/traffic thresholds. Classify
   each link as a **"deep link"** (the linking page stated a specific
   reason for linking — e.g. citing a particular stat or template) or a
   **"general link"** (a vague endorsement) — this determines your
   outreach angle in step 4. Check Authority Score/Domain Rating,
   contextual relevance, and use the Wayback Machine to see what the
   dead page originally said.
3. **Create replacement content.** Study the original via Wayback
   Machine to match its structure and cover the specific points that
   "deep linkers" referenced by name. Then improve on it — updated
   stats, clearer explanations, added templates — so it's a strict
   upgrade, not just a substitute.
4. **Execute outreach.**
   - Find a **named** contact — a specific staff writer or author, not
     a generic inbox — via bylines, social profiles, or contact-finder
     tools.
   - Segment your pitch by the deep-link/general-link classification
     from step 2: deep-link prospects get a pitch referencing their
     specific original reason for linking; general-link prospects get a
     more templated "we noticed this is broken, here's a good
     replacement" pitch.
   - Keep it concise: reference the broken link, explain the
     replacement's value, include the hyperlink directly.
   - Follow up once, after ~3 business days, if there's no response.

## Core insight

"Link building tactics are just streamlined ways of finding link
prospects with a reason for contact" ([[ahrefs-broken-link-building]])
— a 404 error *is* the reason for contact. The tactic's real leverage
isn't anything special about dead links; it's that you're never
starting outreach cold.

## Scale reference

Example from [[ahrefs-broken-link-building]]: Content Marketing
Institute alone had 134 dead pages, some carrying 50+ referring
domains — illustrates how much unclaimed opportunity typically exists
even on a single well-linked competitor site.

## Checklist

- [ ] Prospect list built from at least two discovery tactics
      (competitor 404s, topic-wide search, competitor outbound-broken-
      links, or resource-page mining).
- [ ] Each prospect classified deep-link vs. general-link.
- [ ] Minimum quality bar applied (dofollow, DR/traffic threshold,
      non-toxic).
- [ ] Replacement content matches or exceeds the original via Wayback
      Machine comparison.
- [ ] Named contact identified per prospect (not a generic inbox).
- [ ] Outreach pitch is segmented by deep-link/general-link
      classification.
- [ ] One follow-up sent after ~3 business days if no response.
