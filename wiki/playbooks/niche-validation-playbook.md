# Niche Validation Playbook

**Why / when to use this:** run before committing to a niche for a new
site, product line, or content program, and before
[[keyword-research]]. It prevents building content for a niche with no
buyers, or one where AI answers are already locked up by a few brands.
Concept background: [[niche-selection-and-validation]].

## 1. Define the candidate

For each idea write one sentence each for: the **buyer**, the **painful
trigger**, the **offer**, and the **proof channel**. Discard any idea
where a line is vague ([[surnex-finding-a-niche]]).

- [ ] Buyer describable in one sentence.
- [ ] Problem painful enough to trigger action.
- [ ] Not a bare category label ("Fitness"); narrowed to a "space
      within the space" ([[hubspot-niche-market]]).

## 2. Mine buyer language

Sources: review sites (G2, Capterra), sales calls, support tickets,
autocomplete and People Also Ask, communities (Reddit, Quora, Facebook
groups). See [[semrush-niche-keyword-research]] for the community and
competitor steps and [[keyword-research]] for first-party data mining.

## 3. Quick commercial screen

- [ ] Paid ads appear for niche queries (signal only; the claim that ads
      prove viability is weakly sourced).
- [ ] Specific pain language in the results.
- [ ] Visible content gaps in the SERP.
- [ ] Competitor pricing exists to compare (Amazon for products, G2 for
      software, agency directories for services).
- [ ] AI check: which sources do AI Overviews and assistants cite for
      niche prompts? Thin or outdated sources = opening; a few dominant
      brands = lower attractiveness ([[surnex-finding-a-niche]]).

## 4. Four-week research sprint

- **Weeks 1-2:** collect keywords (Semrush/Ahrefs/GSC/autocomplete),
  group by intent — problem-aware, solution-aware,
  transaction-adjacent, authority — and review SERPs for who ranks,
  formats, forum presence, and AIO patterns.
- **Week 3:** estimate TAM from the *narrow* audience definition, then
  model realistic share given competition, channel access, and sales
  model.
- **Week 4:** write the approval doc: demand breadth, SERP quality,
  audience reachability, credible revenue path. "There are a lot of
  searches" is not an argument.

Filter niche keywords toward low difficulty (roughly 0-49% in Semrush)
for new sites ([[semrush-keyword-research-2026]]).

## 5. Smoke test

One hypothesis, one-page offer (specific headline, pain-focused copy,
single CTA, fast), small paid campaign. Track commitment actions —
signups, add-to-cart, DMs — not page views. Alternatives: free samples,
trials, crowdfunding ([[hubspot-niche-market]]).

- Clear intent → proceed.
- Interest without action → refine positioning.
- No response → reconsider the niche.
- Test privately where possible so a crowded niche isn't tipped off.

## 6. Match content to monetization

Pick one niche type and first conversion: informational service,
product, or community. Define the supporting content asset and what
*not* to build yet. Avoid top-of-funnel articles with no commercial
bridge, and don't mix monetization models before one works
([[surnex-finding-a-niche]]). Affiliate-site benchmarks, with caveats,
in [[diggity-empire-flippers-affiliate-niches]].

## Checklist

- [ ] One-sentence buyer, trigger, offer, proof channel written.
- [ ] Buyer language gathered from at least 3 source types.
- [ ] Commercial and AI-citation screens done.
- [ ] TAM and realistic share modeled.
- [ ] Smoke test run with commitment metrics, decision recorded.
- [ ] First conversion and first content asset defined.
