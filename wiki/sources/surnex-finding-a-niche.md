Surnex Editorial, "Finding a Niche: A Framework for SEO & Product Fit"
(surnex.io, 2026-07-03). Raw copy is a fetch-tool paraphrase, not
verbatim. Vendor blog; several statistics are cited from secondary
sources.

## Key takeaways

- Thesis: a niche is a commercial bet on a specific, painful problem for
  a describable buyer, not a passion project. "Fitness" and "Pet care"
  are containers, not niches. Weak-to-strong examples: "small
  businesses" → "multi-location dental practices".
- Four filter questions: painful enough to trigger action? buyer
  describable in one sentence? commercial signals visible? can the
  topic earn trust in AI-mediated discovery?
- Idea sources: review sites (G2, Capterra), sales calls, support
  tickets, autocomplete/People Also Ask, communities. Shortlist by
  writing one sentence each for buyer, trigger, offer, and proof
  channel; kill ideas where a line stays vague.
- Four-week research sprint: weeks 1-2 keywords grouped by intent
  (problem-aware, solution-aware, transaction-adjacent, authority) plus
  SERP review; week 3 TAM from the narrow audience, then realistic share.
- Validation by smoke test: one-page offer plus small paid campaign,
  measuring commitment actions (signups, add-to-cart, DMs), not page
  views. Test privately to avoid exposing a crowded niche.
- Niche types and monetization: informational service, product,
  community; don't mix models before one proves out.
- AI-search check: AIO representation, entity clarity, citation gaps,
  assistant discoverability. A niche that looks crowded in classic
  SERPs may have thin AI-cited sources (opening); one that compresses
  into a few AI-cited brands is less attractive.
- Weaker claims: "Google ads signal viability" is attributed to a
  community discussion; 68% of consumers use AI assistants for product
  research and the UK higher-ed 95% niche growth figure (2010-2015)
  come from secondary sources.
