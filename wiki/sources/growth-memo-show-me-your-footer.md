# Show me your footer, I show you mine (Kevin Indig)

**Citation:** Indig, Kevin. "Show me your footer, I show you mine."
Growth Memo. Published 2023-07-31.
https://www.growth-memo.com/p/show-me-your-footer-i-show-you-mine

The most substantive of the three footer sources ingested together — it
is the only one that grounds its claims in a named Google statement, a
first-party case, and third-party UX research. See also
[[seoptimer-website-footer-seo]] and
[[heydaymarketing-footer-optimization]].

## Key takeaways

- **Framing: "low impact, but not no impact."** Indig is explicit that
  footer optimization won't double organic traffic. His argument is
  cost-based, not impact-based: it's cheap, low-risk, improves UX, and
  most organizations don't defend the footer politically — so the
  question is "why not?" rather than "why?"
- **The mechanism is crawl, not ranking weight.** A page linked from
  every page on the site (including the homepage) gets crawled more
  often, and more-frequently-crawled pages tend to coincide with
  better-ranking pages. Ties directly to [[how-google-search-works]]'s
  crawling stage.
- **John Mueller: Google does not discount footer links.** Quoted
  directly: "We don't really differentiate there… It's not the case
  that we would say, Oh, like links in a footer have less weight or are
  not as useful… when it comes to links we essentially just see them as
  links on a page," and "whether they're in the header or in the footer
  or the sidebar or the main content, that doesn't really change
  anything for us." This **conflicts with the reasonable-surfer
  placement hierarchy** currently in
  [[link-and-anchor-text-best-practices]] — see that page's Conflicting
  Evidence section.
- **Indig's own reconciliation of that conflict**: the reasonable-surfer
  patent was filed in **2004**; Google registered a **User-Sensitive
  PageRank** patent in **2016** that factors user behavior and trust
  into link weights. He treats reasonable surfer as dated rather than
  wrong-in-principle.
- **G2 case (first-party, uncontrolled)**: optimizing the footer
  "boosted our crawl rate, and our ranks climbed over time." Incremental
  organic traffic attributable to the change: explicitly unknown. Honest
  about being a non-isolated result — treat as directional, not
  measured.
- **Users do reach footers** (Nielsen Norman Group). Scrolling to the
  bottom of a page *signals interest*, making the footer a
  high-intent-but-low-aggression placement surface.
- **"Fat but not obese."** More links than most sites use is fine, as
  long as PageRank and **CheiRank** (the reverse-graph centrality
  measure) stay roughly in balance. The most common mistake he sees is a
  footer containing *only* utility links.
- **Governance point (organizational, not technical)**: SEO teams should
  claim ownership of the footer. It is a viable workaround when other
  teams block changes to the top navigation.
- **Avoid duplicating top-nav links** in the footer — spend the surface
  on pages that aren't already linked.
- **Layout options**: fat footer, double footer, slim+fat combination,
  dropdowns to compress link count, headings for scannability, and
  **contextual footers** that vary by site section (Atlassian's Agile
  microsite vs. main site cited as the example).
- **What to link**: pages not in the top nav, moneymaker guides,
  sub-categories, tools/templates, utility links, sibling brands, an
  **HTML sitemap**, and NN/g "secondary tasks" (careers, investor
  relations, product documentation, media kits/PR, newsletter and social
  links — with social possibly homepage-only).
- **Visual structuring devices**: "new"/"popular" labels, testimonials,
  awards, trust badges, icons.
- **He disputes the "big footers are bad UX" claim** — says he has never
  seen proof of it; rigid organization and visual structure prevent
  overwhelm. This is directly opposed to
  [[seoptimer-website-footer-seo]]'s "simplicity is the best way
  forward."
- **Timing**: do footer work in the "last 20%" — after the fundamentals
  are done well — or earlier if you're diagnosing crawl-rate/crawl-depth
  problems.
- **10 named exemplar footers**: Wethos, Popupsmart, Living Cozy, Stahl
  Kitchens, Open Phone, Atlassian, Ramp, Rippling, Apploye (and
  Typeform), Folk.

## Caveats

- The crawl-frequency → ranking link is stated as a correlation
  ("tend to coincide"), and the supporting citation is an unnamed
  "(source)" link not resolvable from the archived page.
- The G2 result is a single uncontrolled first-party case with no
  isolated traffic measurement — Indig says so himself.
- Mueller's statement is about *how Google treats links it finds*, which
  is not the same as evidence that a footer link is as valuable in
  practice as an in-content link.
