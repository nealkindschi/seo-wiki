# Chunkability: Why Some Content Gets Cited and Most Doesn't (Lumar)

**Citation:** Lumar Editorial Team. "Chunkability: Why Some Content
Gets Cited and Most Doesn't." Lumar Blog. Published 2026-08-24.
https://www.lumar.io/blog/best-practice/chunkability-ai-search-visibility/

## Key takeaways

- **"Chunkability"** = how well a passage functions independently when
  separated from the rest of its page — whether it carries enough
  context to be retrieved, understood, and safely reused in an AI
  answer without the rest of the page.
- **Operates at the citation-selection stage, not eligibility.**
  Content can be topically relevant and still fail to get cited if its
  specific passages lack independence — a distinct filter layered on
  top of retrieval-eligibility gates covered elsewhere in the wiki.
- **Good writing ≠ chunkable writing.** A page that reads well
  holistically can still contain passages that don't survive being
  lifted out of context.
- **Four structural pillars**: Topical Cohesion (one clear
  topic/answer per chunk), Boundary Clarity (clean start/end, complete
  idea containment), Answer Density (specific entities/units/
  constraints/steps, no filler), Self-Contained Meaning (no pronouns or
  references that depend on surrounding page content).
- **Low-chunkability anti-patterns**: long throat-clearing
  introductions before the actual answer, multiple topics crammed into
  one section, instructions split across separate headings, tables
  that need external explanation to make sense.
- **Tactical fixes (edits, not rewrites)**: split multi-topic sections
  into narrowly-scoped passages; move key facts earlier/into the
  relevant passage; structure sections around explicit questions;
  replace pronouns with explicit entity names.
