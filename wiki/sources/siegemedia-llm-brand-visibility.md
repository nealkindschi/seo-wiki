# How To Control What LLMs Say About Your Brand in Three Steps (Siege Media)

**Citation:** Smith, Eli. "How To Control What LLMs Say About Your
Brand in Three Steps." Siege Media. Last updated 2026-05-19.
https://www.siegemedia.com/seo/llm-brand-visibility

## Key takeaways

- **A concrete "multi-vote" three-step audit method** (prompt audit →
  source audit → descriptor audit) that operationalizes the wiki's
  existing sentiment/consistency guidance into a repeatable process:
  run 10-20 buyer-intent prompts across ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini,
  categorize every third-party citation by type, then tally exact
  descriptor phrases across the top 5-10 sources to find consensus
  gaps.
- **Descriptor consistency, not mention volume, is the real target
  metric**: "most organizations measure AI mentions when the right
  metric is descriptive consistency" — being mentioned inconsistently
  across sources ("best value," "most affordable," "budget-friendly"
  for the same brand) fails to build the model's confidence the way
  one consistent descriptor does.
- **Concrete visibility/sentiment benchmarks**: Great = 75-80%
  visibility, 70-75% sentiment, positions 1-2; Good = 55-75%
  visibility, 65-70% sentiment, positions 2-3; Needs Improvement =
  below 40% visibility. A usable scorecard where the wiki's existing
  guidance was mostly qualitative.
- **Proprietary data earns 45% more AI citations than generic "best
  overall" positioning** — a specific number for the wiki's existing
  "originality"/proprietary-data guidance.
- **Zapier case study**: anchoring editorial around four consistent
  descriptors (automation, workflows, integrations, operational
  efficiency) produced a $7.2M traffic increase and 290% organic
  traffic lift.
- **ChatGPT drives 83% of LLM activity** across Siege Media's tracked
  sites — a different metric (their own client-site referral tracking)
  than [[riseatseven-aeo-ai-search-statistics-2026]]'s ~53% "share of
  generative-AI traffic" figure (a broader, differently-sourced
  industry-wide measurement) — not a conflict, but worth citing with
  its own methodology caveat rather than as the same number.
