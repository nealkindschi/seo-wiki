# Google Doesn't Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (Ahrefs)

**Citation:** Law, Ryan (contributor: Xibeijia Guan). "Google Doesn't
Punish AI Content; It Punishes Bad Content (331k Pages Studied)."
Ahrefs Blog. Published 2026-07-27.
https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/
(1M top-10 SERP pages across 100,000 searches, June 2026; 150,000 pages
AI-scored via Ahrefs' AI content detector; indexation sample of
100,000; GSC performance data from 80,861 live URLs across two
six-month windows.)

## Key takeaways

- **Empirically confirms the existing wiki's Scaled Content Abuse
  framing** (see [[e-e-a-t-and-page-quality]]) rather than complicating
  it: Google's stated policy already says scaled *low-effort* content
  is the target "no matter how it's created," not AI content per se —
  this study is direct large-scale evidence that the policy is applied
  that way in practice, not as a blanket AI penalty.
- **AI-written content ranks at every position, including top-3**: 5.3%
  of top-3 pages are entirely AI-generated; 9% of top-3 have ≥80% AI
  content. No "hard cutoff" pattern exists — 40% of very-high-AI-content
  pages remain indexed despite a lower indexation rate (40.35% vs.
  49.28% for low-AI-content pages).
- **Performance gap tracks quality signals, not an AI-detection
  threshold**: low/moderate-AI pages get 2-3x more impressions than
  high/very-high-AI pages, but high-AI pages show *stable* impressions
  over time rather than a cliff-edge drop — inconsistent with a binary
  "Google detected AI and demoted it" mechanism, consistent with a
  gradual quality-correlate mechanism instead.
- **Names the specific quality issues that commonly co-occur with AI
  content** (not caused by AI-ness itself): repetition of common
  knowledge without new value, missing internal/external links/images,
  academic writing without personality, factual errors — a concrete
  list of what to check for in AI-assisted drafts, more specific than
  the wiki's existing "genuine added value, editing, and originality"
  guidance.
- **The "Mount AI" traffic-spike-then-crash pattern explained via crawl
  economics, not an AI penalty**: Google may burst-crawl a newly scaled
  site out of curiosity, but throttles resource allocation once it's
  clear the domain lacks baseline authority to sustain that scale —
  reframes a widely-assumed "Google caught the AI content" narrative as
  an authority/crawl-budget mechanism instead.
- **Author's explicit synthesis**: quality problems "commonly co-occur
  with AI use, but they are not guaranteed by AI use" — advocates
  neither fear of AI-assisted content nor a free pass, specifically
  warning against spam/scaled content as the actual target.

## Relationship to existing wiki claims

- **Agrees with and empirically extends** [[e-e-a-t-and-page-quality]]'s
  Scaled Content Abuse section, which already states Google's policy
  targets low-effort automated content "no matter how it's created,"
  not AI-authorship per se. This study is the large-scale empirical
  confirmation that policy claim was missing — previously the wiki only
  had Google's stated policy language, not outcome data testing it.
- **Sharpens** [[geo-content-optimization-tactics]]'s "Critical warning
  — Scaled Content Abuse" section (which already warns AI drafting
  requires genuine editorial work) with concrete, checkable failure
  modes (missing links/images, academic tone, factual errors) instead
  of only the abstract "add genuine value" guidance.
- **Contextualizes** [[ahrefs-b2b-seo-statistics-2025]]'s 87%-of-
  marketers-use-AI / 4.7x-lower-cost / 42%-higher-volume stat (already
  flagged in the wiki as "the exact volume/cost dynamic that makes
  Scaled Content Abuse easy to fall into") — this study shows that
  dynamic doesn't uniformly tank rankings, only when quality genuinely
  suffers, softening but not contradicting the existing caution.
- **No conflicts** with any existing wiki claim.
