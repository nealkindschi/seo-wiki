# Why Ranking on Page One Isn't Enough (AirOps)

**Citation:** Oshen Davidson. "Why Ranking on Page One Isn't Enough."
AirOps. Published 2025-07-18.
https://www.airops.com/report/structuring-content-for-llms
(900 high-intent queries, 15 industries, 12,000+ URLs — ChatGPT-cited
URLs vs. Google page-one results for identical queries, off-page
factors deliberately excluded.)

## Key takeaways

- **Almost certainly the underlying study behind
  [[airops-geo-strategy-playbook]]'s "12,000+ pages" structural stats**
  (published a year later, 2026-07-21): that report's "schema 3+ types
  +13%" and "heading hierarchy 2.8x higher odds" figures match this
  source's headline numbers exactly. This source is the more granular,
  original dataset — treat it as the primary citation for those two
  stats going forward, with the later report as independent
  confirmation the numbers held up across a follow-up analysis.
- **Schema breakdown**: 61% of ChatGPT-cited pages carried rich schema
  (3+ types) vs. 25% of Google page-one URLs for the same queries; FAQ/
  QAPage schema specifically: 10.5% vs. 5.4%.
- **Heading breakdown**: 68.7% of cited pages had sequential H1→H2→H3
  structure (no skipped levels) vs. 23.9% of Google leaders; 87% had a
  single H1 vs. 64%.
- **Lists are one of the most decisive differentiators measured**: 79%
  of cited pages had at least one list section vs. 28.6% of Google
  results, and cited pages averaged 13.75 list sections vs. Google's
  average — over 17x higher.
- **Bullet points are NOT a differentiator**, contrary to what the
  list/schema/heading findings might suggest: 72.9% of cited pages had
  bullet sections vs. 74.5% of Google results — essentially
  identical. Sentence length was also nearly identical (17.32 vs. 16.54
  characters) — structure (headings, schema, ordered lists) drives the
  gap, not sentence-level simplicity or bullet-point presence alone.
- **Forums are heavily present in Google but nearly absent from ChatGPT
  citations for the same queries**: Reddit/Quora made up 11.1% of
  Google page-one results but only 0.05% of ChatGPT citations — a much
  more extreme version of the "Reddit retrieved but rarely cited"
  pattern already in [[ai-citation-landscape]], now specifically
  measured as a Google-vs-ChatGPT gap on identical queries rather than
  ChatGPT's citation rate alone.
- Methodology deliberately excludes off-page/authority factors, isolating
  on-page structure as the variable — strengthens the causal read
  ("controlling for ranking position and query, structure differs
  systematically") over prior wiki correlational findings.
