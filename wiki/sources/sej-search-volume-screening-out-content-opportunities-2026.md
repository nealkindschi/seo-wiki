# Why Search Volume Is Screening Out Your Best Content Opportunities (SEJ)

**Citation:** Blauer, Itamar. "Why Search Volume Is Screening Out Your
Best Content Opportunities." Search Engine Journal. Published
2026-08-18.
https://www.searchenginejournal.com/why-search-volume-is-screening-out-your-best-content-opportunities/585048/
(Opinion/practitioner analysis, no quantified study data — argues from
the mechanics of AI query fan-out and illustrative examples, not an
empirical dataset.)

## Key takeaways

- **Search volume only counts typed queries**, missing the long,
  conversational prompts people use with AI assistants — e.g. "best crm
  for small business" (high volume) vs. "We're a 12-person agency
  outgrowing spreadsheets. What CRM should we move to?" (zero
  measurable volume, same underlying buyer need). Query fan-out means a
  page can be cited for sub-questions the literal prompt never
  mentioned.
- **The paradox**: teams end up prioritizing whichever queries are
  easiest to count, not the ones that address real buyer needs.
- **Four alternative prioritization approaches**: target sub-questions
  over head terms (8-10 foundational buyer questions); prioritize
  entities/concepts over keyword repetition; address decisions
  (comparisons, trade-offs, selection criteria) over definitions; keep
  using volume where it still works (brand, transactional, local/"near
  me" queries).
- **Alternative scoring signals to replace volume**: business value
  (does it move prospects toward a purchase decision?), evidence of
  demand (PAA appearances, forum discussions, recurring sales-call
  questions), coverage gaps (strengthen existing pages vs. new pages),
  answerability (can it be addressed with specific, concrete
  information?).
- **Topic-discovery sources**: PAA/related searches, Reddit/Quora/forums,
  sales calls and support tickets, and directly testing prompts against
  AI assistants.
- **Measurement caveat, stated by the author**: track AI-referral
  traffic and citation pages plus organic long-tail ranking
  accumulation, but "assistants don't give you that data (yet)" — treat
  this as directional, not a precise ROI model.
- **Not an argument against keyword research** — a call to stop
  treating volume as the sole/primary arbiter of content priority,
  because AI synthesis is "far less forgiving" of incomplete topic
  coverage than traditional SERPs.
