# Does Topical Focus Make Your Brand More Visible? (Growth Memo)

**Citation:** Indig, Kevin. "Does topical focus make your brand more
visible?" Growth Memo. Published 2026-08-03.
https://www.growth-memo.com/p/does-topical-focus-make-your-brand
(Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit, US ChatGPT, Jan-June 2026; 1,094
categories, 5 prompt variants each, 283,215 citation observations,
76,493 named-mention observations, 45,578 expansion appearances by
1,458 brand entities.)

## Key takeaways

- **Sharpens the existing citations-vs-mentions distinction in
  [[ai-citation-landscape]] with quantitative topical-relevance data**:
  citation eligibility is largely topic-agnostic (41% citation-only in
  distant categories vs. 40% in close ones — essentially flat), but
  named-mention/recommendation share is strongly topic-gated (25%
  mention rate in distant categories vs. 44% in close ones). "You can
  be a source on pretty much any topic... but the AI recommends you in
  core topics" — a precise, measured version of the wiki's existing
  "citations ≠ recommendation" framing.
- **Depth beats breadth, and the "spread-thin penalty" is really a
  shallow-presence penalty**: appearing in many categories with only
  1-of-5 prompt-variant depth associates *negatively* with brand
  mentions (-0.051), reversing to slightly positive at full depth
  (5-of-5). Citation share barely penalizes breadth at all
  (+0.012 to +0.062 across breadth levels) — the risk isn't covering
  many topics, it's covering them shallowly.
- **Industry variation is large and directly actionable**: Finance and
  Real Estate reward broad citation coverage (breadth association rises
  from +0.054→+0.139 and +0.021→+0.135 respectively), while Legal and
  Healthcare punish breadth for mentions specifically (staying negative,
  -0.058 and -0.037, even at full coverage) — "being a credible source
  is not enough to make a brand the recommended answer" in high-stakes/
  YMYL-adjacent fields.
- **A concrete category-ownership threshold**: "A category is not won
  until the brand earns repeat mentions, not just citations" — citation
  presence is necessary but explicitly insufficient for category
  ownership.
- **A light, measured expansion effect**: brands with 3+/5 prompt-
  variant depth in an established category get a measurable but light
  advantage expanding into related categories — supports a "go deep in
  one category first, then expand to adjacent ones" sequencing.

## Relationship to existing wiki claims

- **Extends and quantifies** [[ai-citation-landscape]]'s "Citations vs.
  brand mentions" section (from
  [[similarweb-how-to-be-the-brand-ai-recommends-2026]]), which already
  distinguishes citations (retrieval-sourced) from mentions
  (training-data-or-retrieval-sourced, recommendation-flavored) but had
  no topical-relevance breakdown. This source adds the missing
  quantitative layer: citation eligibility is topic-agnostic, mention/
  recommendation eligibility is topic-gated.
- **Extends** [[growth-memo-topics-matter-for-third-party-authority]]
  (same author, 2026-06-15) — that source established that *third-party
  source trust* is topic-specific; this source shows the same
  topic-specificity applies to *first-party brand recommendation*
  (mentions), via a much larger, differently-sourced dataset
  (Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit vs. that source's original dataset).
- **Extends** [[ai-visibility-correlation-factors]]'s "content volume
  has near-zero correlation with AI visibility" finding with a sharper
  mechanism: it's not that volume/breadth is neutral, it's that breadth
  without depth is actively negative for mentions while breadth *with*
  depth is fine-to-positive — reconciles why "more content" studies
  sometimes show no effect (they're not distinguishing shallow from
  deep breadth).
- **No conflicts** — this is a new, complementary quantitative layer on
  an already-established conceptual distinction, not a contradiction of
  any prior claim.
