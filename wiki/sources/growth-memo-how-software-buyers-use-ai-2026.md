# How Software Buyers Use AI (Growth Memo)

**Citation:** Indig, Kevin. "How software buyers use AI." Growth Memo.
Published 2026-11-09.
https://www.growth-memo.com/p/how-software-buyers-use-ai
(Underlying data: a 13-question Wynter survey of 200 software buyers who
shared the first prompt and follow-ups from a real AI-assisted software
purchase. The author is an investor in Wynter.)

## Key takeaways

- **AI expands shortlists.** 79% (158/200) had at least one vendor in
  mind before the chat. Of those, 62.7% (99) added another vendor
  through AI. Of the 40 who started with no vendors, AI put a
  shortlisted vendor in front of 33 (82.5%), but only 40% of that group
  went on to choose a vendor.
- **Starting with vendors in mind correlates with action.** Demo/signup
  was 36.1% for buyers with candidates vs. 25.0% for buyers without.
- **First-answer inclusion matters but doesn't close the deal.** Of 126
  buyers who chose a vendor, 62% (78) said it was in the AI's first
  answer and 34% (43) said it appeared only after follow-ups. 75 of the
  78 still sent follow-ups. Demo/signup was 41.0% vs. 34.9%, which the
  author says is not a causal effect.
- **Chats are evaluation sessions.** 41.5% sent 3–5 follow-ups and
  one-third sent 6+. Follow-up topics: pricing 57.5%, vendor
  comparisons 54.5%, integrations 46%, setup 40%. Topic breadth rises
  with length (2.4 topics at 1–2 follow-ups, 5.0 at 10+). Buyers use
  follow-ups to eliminate vendors (e.g. "drop anything without a
  Marketo API integration").
- **Longer chats correlate with more demos, up to a point.**
  Demo/signup was 41.8% at 6+ follow-ups vs. 32.3% at 1–5, peaking at
  6–10 and falling at 10+.
- **AI complements Google rather than replacing it.** After the chat:
  vendor websites 72.5%, Google 48.0%, peers 31.0%, Reddit 29.0%,
  G2/review sites 23.0%.
- **Buyers use several assistants.** 67.5% used more than one. Claude
  71%, ChatGPT 62.5%, Gemini 38.5%, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode 15%.
  Demo/signup by tool was similar (Claude 35.2%, ChatGPT 31.2%, Gemini
  33.8%) and the groups overlap.
- **Most chats start cold.** 68.5% selected "fresh chat, no extra
  context" (52.5% selected only that). 26% used projects/saved
  instructions and 21% uploaded files. Account memory wasn't measured.
- **Author's recommendations:** build familiarity before buyers are in
  market (ads, creators, social, email), earn consideration in AI
  answers once research starts, and make pricing, integrations and
  product fit easy to evaluate. Mention counts alone are an incomplete
  measure. Frames this with his "retrieved → cited → trusted" pipeline.

## Caveats

Self-reported, unweighted and not representative of all B2B buyers. The
sample is self-selected AI users. Prompts were recalled from memory. A
demo/signup isn't a purchase. The survey can't show whether an
AI-introduced vendor was the one chosen, and chat length and
first-answer inclusion are correlations. No fieldwork dates. Chart
images were not viewed during ingest; figures come from the article
text.

## Relationship to existing wiki claims

- **Agrees with** [[ahrefs-b2b-seo-statistics-2025]]: 81% of B2B buyers
  have a preferred vendor before first outreach and 71% choose their
  first shortlisted option. This study's 79% and 62% point the same way
  from a different, AI-specific angle.
- **Tension (not filed as a conflict) with** [[ai-traffic-scale-vs-hype]]:
  that page argues standalone AI tools are tiny in clickstream terms and
  Google-embedded AI is the bigger threat. Here, among software buyers
  who chose to use AI, Claude and ChatGPT far outrank AI Overviews/AI
  Mode (15%). Different populations and measures (whole-web traffic vs.
  a self-selected buyer sample), so both can be true.
- **Extends** [[ai-mediated-commercial-persuasion]] with real-world
  evidence that chat interfaces shape B2B shortlists, though not whether
  that steering is sponsored.
