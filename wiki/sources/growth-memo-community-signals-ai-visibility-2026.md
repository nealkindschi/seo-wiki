# How to Grow Your AI Visibility with Community Signals (Kevin Indig / Amanda Johnson, Growth Memo)

**Citation:** Indig, Kevin; Johnson, Amanda. "How to Grow Your AI
Visibility with Community Signals." Growth Memo. Published 2026-08-10.
https://www.growth-memo.com/p/community-signals-are-ais-largest
(Methodology: ~35,000 citation URLs via Profound; US-only, ChatGPT
only, December 2025; 376 discovery / 1,179 exploration / 1,367
evaluation / 255 focused-evaluation prompts; deduplicated one per
run/intent/domain. Companion piece to
[[growth-memo-topics-matter-for-third-party-authority]] and
[[growth-memo-why-most-original-data-never-gets-cited]] by the same
authors.)

## Key takeaways

- **UGC citations hold a flat ~17-18% floor across the entire buyer
  journey** (17.1% of cited domains excluding vendor domains) — unlike
  review platforms, which spike specifically during evaluation stages.
  UGC alone is ~4x the size of the publisher share (4.0%).
- **Wikipedia, Reddit, and LinkedIn = 99% of UGC citations** — but
  individual platforms are highly volatile even while the aggregate
  stays stable: Wikipedia alone accounts for 10.1-14.0 of the ~17
  points.
- **Platform-specific behavior**: Wikipedia is the largest UGC source
  but least actionable (needs accurate upstream sourcing for editors);
  Reddit is the most volatile (-11.7% visibility/-10.9% mentions in one
  28-day window, +18.1% recovery three weeks later) but worth
  prioritizing; LinkedIn rewards named individuals over brand accounts
  (43.3% visibility gain in one period); YouTube is comparatively
  durable (Google-owned, more controllable, +11% growth).
- **Freshness**: 83% of commercial-query citations come from pages
  updated in the past 12 months; stale pages are 3x more likely to
  lose citations.
- **Structural finding**: sequential heading hierarchy (H2>H3>H4, no
  skipped levels) → 2.8x citation increase.
- **Brand visibility is highly volatile run-to-run**: only 30% of
  brand visibility persists between separate AI responses to
  similar/repeated prompts.
- **Practical distinction**: separate the "aggregate UGC floor" (~17%,
  the number to defend) from "platform composition" (the specific
  Wikipedia/Reddit/LinkedIn mix, which should be rebalanced
  quarterly) — category visibility is portfolio-level, not a
  single-platform bet.
- **Eight actionable tactics**: identify category-feeding UGC platforms
  via high-intent prompt testing; highlight named people over brand
  accounts; answer recurring support-ticket questions on community
  platforms; embed third-party review quotes (with links) on top-cited
  pages; track the aggregate floor and rebalance composition quarterly;
  mine communities for audience terminology; correct outdated thread
  info with dated updates; provide customer-worthy shareable assets.
- **Measurement gap**: Google Search Console's July 2026 "platform
  properties" feature covers Instagram/TikTok/X/YouTube but excludes
  Reddit and LinkedIn — the two highest-performing UGC platforms in
  this analysis.
- **Methodology caveats acknowledged by the authors**: single-engine
  (ChatGPT-only) analysis; the 91%-single-engine "consensus gap"
  documented elsewhere in this wiki means these shares don't generalize
  automatically to other engines; prompts were vendor-seeking by design.

- **Reddit/UGC prominence in ChatGPT**: this source finds a much larger
  ChatGPT UGC share (17.1%, of which Wikipedia is the majority) than
  [[brightedge-ai-search-same-brands-different-sources]]'s 0.5% ChatGPT
  UGC figure cited in [[ai-citation-landscape]]'s existing unresolved
  Reddit-prominence conflict. **Not added to that conflict directly** —

  "UGC"; Wikipedia alone is 10.1-14 of this source's 17.1 points, so
  Reddit+LinkedIn's actual combined share here may be closer to
  BrightEdge's low figure). Noted as a related data point, not folded
