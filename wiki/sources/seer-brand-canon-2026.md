# What Is AI Saying About Your Brand? Develop a Brand Canon and Find Out (Seer Interactive)

**Citation:** Lovett, John (VP Analytics, Seer Interactive). "What Is
AI Saying About Your Brand? Develop a Brand Canon and Find Out." Seer
Interactive. Published 2026-08-17.
https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/brand-canon
(Methodology: 28,123 responses analyzed across 6 AI models about Seer's
own brand; ~70% factual accuracy, ~30% wrong/outdated/declined.)

## Key takeaways

- **A "brand canon"** = documented ground truth (facts + desired
  positioning), sourced and dated, used as the reference against which
  AI-generated brand statements are checked — distinct from brand
  guidelines (visual) or a style guide (language).
- **~70% accuracy baseline**: across 28,123 responses/6 models, ~30% of
  AI statements about the brand were wrong, outdated, or a decline/
  non-answer. "Visibility without accuracy is being wrong at scale."
- **No standardized measurement currently tracks both visibility and
  accuracy together** — most brand-monitoring tooling covers one or the
  other, not both simultaneously.
- **Three-layer framework**: Canon (specification: sourced/dated true
  claims + positioning) → Claim Inventory (test suite: falsifiable
  assertions tiered by revenue impact) → Accuracy Score (test run:
  pass/fail rates per platform).
- **Five canon categories**: Facts (sourced), Desired positioning
  (declared), Brand description (declared), Differentiators (both),
  Falsehoods to actively watch for — retired products, old taglines,
  competitor confusion (both).
- **Four scoring dimensions**: factual correctness, canon alignment,
  attribution quality (owned vs. third-party vs. competitor source),
  hallucination presence.
- **Two fix timeframes**: retrieval fixes (weeks — update content,
  correct third-party listings, optimize for exact-query matching) vs.
  corpus fixes (quarters — build authoritative web footprint for the
  next training cycle; training data itself can't be directly edited).
- **Human review is required** — model self-assessment of its own
  accuracy isn't statistically reliable.
